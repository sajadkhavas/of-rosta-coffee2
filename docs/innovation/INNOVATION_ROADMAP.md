# ROSTA Innovation Roadmap

Version: **1.0**
Date: **2026-08-20**

## Objective

Convert selected ROSTA capabilities from ordinary product features into defensible technology products through staged R&D, measurable technical progress and commercialization evidence without disrupting core marketplace delivery.

## Program structure

ROSTA innovation runs as a cross-cutting track. It must not hijack critical commerce, finance, compliance or production-readiness work.

Each technology track progresses only when prior gates are satisfied.

---

# Stage 0 — Foundation & Evidence Discipline

## Goal

Ensure every future R&D activity is attributable, reproducible and legally usable.

## Required outputs

- technology-product boundaries locked;
- R&D evidence template active;
- dataset registry policy active;
- model/algorithm registry policy active;
- IP chain-of-title policy active;
- privacy/security review trigger defined;
- financial revenue/cost tagging contract defined;
- ADR process used for material technical decisions.

## Exit gate

No R&D candidate may enter Stage 1 without an owner, problem statement, baseline and measurable technical metric.

---

# Stage 1 — Taste Intelligence R&D

## 1A. Baseline definition

Freeze current deterministic recommendation behavior as a benchmarkable baseline.

Evidence:
- exact implementation SHA;
- input schema;
- ranking rules;
- inventory/availability constraints;
- evaluation fixtures;
- known limitations.

## 1B. Taste representation experiments

Candidate work:
- normalized sensory feature representation;
- explicit preference encoding;
- product/customer similarity models;
- confidence for sparse profiles;
- cold-start strategy;
- explainable reason codes.

Do not introduce ML merely for complexity. Compare against deterministic baseline.

## 1C. Feedback learning

Add controlled use of:
- verified review outcomes;
- reorder behavior;
- preference edits;
- skip/dislike signals;
- subscription acceptance where available.

Privacy and bias review required.

## 1D. Online validation

Only after offline evaluation passes:
- shadow ranking;
- limited cohort rollout;
- A/B or interleaving where appropriate;
- rollback threshold;
- monitoring for invalid recommendations and coverage regressions.

## Stage-1 exit

Taste Intelligence reaches a serious candidate state when it has repeatable evidence of technical improvement over the frozen baseline and a separable service boundary.

---

# Stage 2 — Fulfillment Intelligence R&D

## 2A. Operational baseline

Model current policy-based routing:
- direct seller fulfillment;
- ROSTA Hub eligibility;
- grinding requirement;
- shipment legs;
- cut-offs;
- carrier/local delivery.

Build deterministic replay/simulation fixtures.

## 2B. Cost/SLA model

Create measurable estimates for:
- carrier cost;
- handling cost;
- hub processing time;
- SLA risk;
- batch/consolidation effect;
- capacity utilization.

## 2C. Optimizer candidates

Compare increasingly sophisticated methods, for example:
- rules + weighted scoring;
- constrained optimization;
- heuristics/metaheuristics where justified;
- predictive ETA/risk components where adequate data exists.

Selection depends on evidence, not novelty.

## 2D. Safe decisioning

Production decision service must include:
- deterministic input snapshot;
- explanation/reason code;
- timeout budget;
- manual/policy fallback;
- replayability;
- no silent routing when essential provider data is unavailable.

## Stage-2 exit

Demonstrated reduction in a defined cost/SLA objective versus baseline without unacceptable operational risk.

---

# Stage 3 — Traceability & Quality Intelligence R&D

## 3A. Trace graph

Connect existing operational truth:
- roast batch;
- inventory movement;
- seller sub-order;
- chain of custody;
- grinding/QC;
- packaging;
- shipment legs;
- delivery;
- verified feedback/incidents.

Measure trace completeness rather than assuming it.

## 3B. Freshness intelligence

Start with transparent rule-based freshness metrics. Introduce statistical/predictive approaches only with suitable labels and measurable improvement.

## 3C. Quality anomaly detection

Research signals across:
- complaint clusters;
- review changes;
- batch identity;
- processing/QC events;
- shipping delays;
- seller/hub patterns.

Protect against low-volume false positives.

## Stage-3 exit

Traceability is operationally reliable and at least one intelligence output has validated technical/quality value.

---

# Stage 4 — Independent Productization

A technology candidate is not an independently commercialized product until ROSTA defines:

- product/service name;
- customer persona;
- stable interface/API/workflow;
- SLA/support boundary;
- security/privacy contract;
- pricing/billing basis;
- release/version policy;
- customer/pilot evidence;
- revenue tagging;
- IP ownership package.

Potential examples:

- ROSTA Taste Intelligence API;
- ROSTA Coffee Recommendation Service;
- ROSTA Fulfillment Decision Engine;
- ROSTA Coffee Traceability & Quality Service.

Names are placeholders and do not imply eligibility.

---

# Stage 5 — Company & External Evaluation Readiness

Once a legal ROSTA company exists and one candidate is sufficiently mature:

1. revalidate official knowledge-based criteria and product lists;
2. select exact candidate/evaluation scope;
3. close any IP chain-of-title gaps;
4. prepare live demonstration;
5. prepare R&D evidence bundle;
6. prepare financial/company evidence;
7. prepare product/customer evidence;
8. run an internal adversarial evaluation;
9. obtain qualified legal/accounting review where required;
10. apply only when evidence is complete.

## Parallelization rule

Core product Waves may continue while R&D experiments run, but technology branches may not silently change:

- financial truth;
- order truth;
- seller payout truth;
- fulfillment custody truth;
- privacy/security boundaries;
- production release controls.

Any dependency change follows normal phase ownership and central integration review.

## Annual strategy review

At least annually, evaluate each candidate on:

- technical defensibility;
- product-list/eligibility relevance;
- commercial value;
- data readiness;
- R&D cost;
- team capability;
- IP cleanliness;
- operational risk.

Continue, pivot or retire based on evidence.
