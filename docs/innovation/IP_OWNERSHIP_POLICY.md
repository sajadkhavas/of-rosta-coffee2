# ROSTA IP Ownership & Chain-of-Title Policy

Version: **1.0**  
Date: **2026-08-20**

## Purpose

ROSTA must be able to prove who owns or is authorized to use every material asset behind its technology products. This policy protects future company formation, investment, licensing, commercialization and technical evaluation.

## Covered assets

- source code;
- algorithms and optimization logic;
- model weights created by ROSTA where applicable;
- prompts/workflows only to the extent protectable/contractually controlled;
- datasets and dataset transformations;
- database schemas and domain models;
- technical documentation;
- ADRs and experiment reports;
- UI/UX assets and design systems;
- brand/domain/trademark assets;
- product specifications;
- operational playbooks;
- inventions/patentable subject matter where applicable;
- third-party licenses and provider rights.

## Current-stage rule

Before a ROSTA company exists, contributions must have a documented rights path to the current project owner/operator. The future company must receive the required rights through a written assignment or license at cutover; no assumption that company registration automatically transfers historic IP.

## Contributor classes

### Founder/current owner work

Maintain:
- Git authorship/history;
- original asset/source provenance;
- domain/brand purchase records;
- vendor/account records;
- dated technical documents.

At company formation, execute an IP schedule listing assets transferred or licensed to the company and the effective date.

### Employee work

Future employment agreements must explicitly address, as permitted by applicable law:

- work-product/IP rights;
- confidentiality;
- source/data access;
- return/deletion of company materials;
- open-source obligations;
- invention disclosure;
- use of pre-existing employee IP.

### Contractor/freelancer/vendor work

No critical contributor begins material technology-product work without a written agreement defining:

- deliverables;
- payment;
- ownership/assignment or sufficiently broad license;
- right to modify, commercialize and sublicense where required;
- confidentiality;
- third-party/open-source disclosure;
- prohibition on inserting unlicensed assets;
- repository/account return at termination;
- warranty that contributed material can legally be provided.

Payment alone must not be treated as proof of IP transfer.

### Open-source software

Maintain an OSS inventory/SBOM process. For each dependency track:

- package/version;
- license;
- source;
- modification status;
- redistribution obligations;
- attribution/notice obligations;
- copyleft/network-copyleft implications where relevant.

A technology product must not rely on an unknown-license snippet, model, dataset or copied implementation.

### External AI/model providers

Track separately:

- provider/model ID;
- API/service terms;
- input-data policy;
- output-use terms;
- retention/training controls where available;
- region/availability dependency;
- production fallback;
- whether any fine-tuned/derived artifact is actually owned or only licensed.

Calling a third-party model does not make that model ROSTA IP.

## Dataset rights

A dataset registry must distinguish:

- data generated by ROSTA operations;
- customer personal data under privacy obligations;
- seller-provided data;
- public/open datasets;
- licensed third-party datasets;
- synthetic datasets.

ROSTA's right to process data for operations does not automatically imply unrestricted ownership or the right to publish/license it. Dataset commercialization requires a separate legal/privacy review.

## Brand and domain assets

Maintain an asset register for:

- `rosta.shop` and future domains;
- logos/design files;
- social handles;
- trademark applications/registrations where pursued;
- brand usage licenses.

At company transition, decide explicitly whether each asset is assigned or licensed and update registrant/account control where appropriate.

## Repository and credential control

Critical repositories and service accounts must have organization-grade ownership after company formation. Personal credentials must not remain the sole production control point.

Required controls:

- least-privilege access;
- MFA where supported;
- named owners;
- offboarding procedure;
- no shared personal passwords;
- secrets never committed to Git;
- protected backups of critical configuration/evidence.

## IP register

Create and maintain a private register with at least:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Asset ID | Stable identifier |
| Asset | Code/model/data/brand/document/invention |
| Current rights holder | Person/company/third party |
| Creator/contributors | Provenance |
| Creation/acquisition date | Timeline |
| Repository/evidence ref | Immutable evidence |
| License/assignment basis | Rights proof |
| Restrictions | OSS/privacy/vendor/etc. |
| Intended company treatment | Assign/license/replace |
| Status | clear / review / blocked |

## IP-blocked release rule

A technology-product release is `IP_BLOCKED` if any material component has:

- unknown provenance;
- incompatible license;
- missing contractor rights;
- disputed ownership;
- prohibited dataset use;
- provider terms incompatible with commercialization.

The issue must be resolved or the component replaced before representing the product as company-owned technology.

## Future company transfer package

At cutover prepare:

1. IP assignment/license agreement;
2. attached asset schedule;
3. repository list and SHAs/tags;
4. domains/brand schedule;
5. dataset register;
6. OSS/SBOM report;
7. model/provider register;
8. contractor/employee rights evidence;
9. confidential know-how schedule where appropriate;
10. acceptance record by the company.

Historic Git history remains intact. Legal ownership changes are documented; history is not rewritten.
